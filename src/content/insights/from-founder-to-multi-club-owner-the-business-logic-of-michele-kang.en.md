Before she owned football clubs, she built a company. **Michele Kang's** rise in women's football is often told as a sports story, but it is just as much a business one: a founder-turned-investor applying discipline in structure, scale and long-term value to an asset class many still treat as a passion project.

Sports ownership is often described through emotion: heritage, loyalty, the pull of the badge. Michele Kang's approach is a useful counterexample. Born in Seoul in 1959, she moved abroad to study, earned a degree in economics at the University of Chicago and a master's in public and private management at Yale School of Management, and started out as a management consultant in information technology. In 2008, she founded Cognosante, a health IT company she built from a home start-up into an established business, and sold in 2024.

That path matters, because it explains how she approaches sport: as an operator and a builder of platforms, not only as an owner.

**From one club to a platform**

Kang joined the Washington Spirit's ownership group in 2020 and became its majority owner in 2022. In December 2023 she acquired London City Lionesses, and in February 2024 she completed the purchase of Lyon's women's team. In July 2024, she brought the three together under Kynisca Sports International, the first global multi-team organisation dedicated to women's football.

The logic is familiar from corporate strategy: shared expertise, shared standards and economies of scale across several markets, while each club keeps its own identity, heritage and community. It is the same question MCSC's corporate advisory clients ask when expanding internationally: how do you grow a group without flattening what makes each part valuable?

**Capital, patience and credibility**

The numbers show that the model is built on long-term commitment rather than quick returns. Kang's YMK Holdings put £22.4 million into London City Lionesses across two financial years. The club became the first fully independent team promoted to the top women's league in May 2025, and has since signed what Kang describes as the highest front-of-shirt deal for a women's team, with Nike. Washington Spirit, by her own account, is already approaching break-even.

Here, the commercial lesson is the one at the heart of sport and entertainment investment: building credibility first, then opening new markets. Investment creates visibility; visibility attracts partners; partners create revenue. Each step depends on the previous one.

**Why it matters for investors and entrepreneurs**

For entrepreneurs and families considering sport as part of their portfolio, Kang's story highlights three points. First, structure comes before enthusiasm: a multi-club model needs governance and clear capital allocation from the start. Second, undervalued segments reward patience, and women's football remains early on the commercial curve. Third, cross-border presence is an advantage only when supported by local credibility, in this case clubs rooted in the US, France and the UK.

Ideas may begin as passion, but they endure on discipline, planning and trust.
